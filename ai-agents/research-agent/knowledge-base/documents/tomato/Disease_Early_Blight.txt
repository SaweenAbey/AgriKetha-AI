Crop: Tomato
Category: Disease

2. EARLY
BLIGHT

Scientific cause: Alternaria solani.

Identification / symptoms: - Dark brown spots with concentric rings
forming a target/bullseye pattern, usually on older, lower leaves first.
- Yellow tissue often surrounds each spot. - Sunken, dark lesions can
also appear on stems and near the fruit stem end.

Effects: - Progressive loss of lower leaves reduces photosynthesis and
can expose fruit to sunscald. - Stem lesions can weaken young plants.

Management: 1. Rotate crops away from tomato and potato for 2-3 years.
2. Mulch to reduce soil splash onto leaves. 3. Remove infected lower
leaves promptly. 4. Ensure adequate plant spacing for airflow. 5. Apply
labelled fungicides such as chlorothalonil or mancozeb where locally
approved once symptoms appear.

Recovery: - Existing lesions do not heal, so protect healthy new foliage.
- Correct crowding, mulch coverage, and irrigation practices that keep
leaves wet.

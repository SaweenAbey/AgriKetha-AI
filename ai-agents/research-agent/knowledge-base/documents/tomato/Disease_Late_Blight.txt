Crop: Tomato
Category: Disease

4. LATE
BLIGHT

Scientific cause: Phytophthora infestans.

Identification / symptoms: - Irregular, dark, water-soaked, greasy-looking
lesions on leaves, often starting at leaf edges or tips. - A pale
green-to-yellow halo may surround lesions. - White, fuzzy fungal growth
can appear on the underside of leaves in humid conditions. - Dark, firm,
greasy blotches can develop on fruit.

Effects: - One of the most destructive tomato diseases; it can defoliate
and kill plants within days under cool, wet, humid conditions. - Infected
fruit is unmarketable and infection can continue to spread after harvest.

Management: 1. Act quickly at first sign of symptoms; this disease
spreads fast. 2. Remove and destroy infected plants or plant parts away
from the field. 3. Avoid overhead irrigation and working in wet foliage.
4. Apply labelled protectant fungicides (e.g. chlorothalonil, mancozeb,
or copper products) where locally approved, especially before forecast
cool wet weather. 5. Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available.

Recovery: - Badly affected plants rarely recover; prioritize protecting
unaffected plants and neighboring fields. - Report widespread or rapidly
spreading outbreaks to an agricultural extension officer.
